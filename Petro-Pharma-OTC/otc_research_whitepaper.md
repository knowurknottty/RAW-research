# OTC Formulation Audit: Primary-Source Review of a 50-Row U.S. OTC Worksheet

**Research type:** Adversarial formulation audit  
**Evidentiary standard:** Primary-source product labels (DailyMed priority); adversarial disconfirmation protocol  
**Date of session:** August 25, 2026  
**Status:** Publication-safe synthesis; line-by-line citation trail in session record  

---

## Executive Overview

This document preserves the publication-safe findings from a session-long adversarial audit of a 50-row U.S. over-the-counter drug worksheet. The research question was whether ordinary OTC products repeatedly surface petrochemical-linked active substances, solvents, preservatives, polymers, coating agents, propellants, or related excipients at the product-label level.

The resulting record supports a narrow but strong claim: such ingredients appear widely across the audited OTC landscape. However, the strength of that claim is frequently formulation-specific rather than universal at the molecule level. That distinction is not a weakness — it is the precise scope the evidence allows.

---

## Research Standard

The session applied an adversarial evidentiary standard rather than a thesis-protective one. The work prioritized primary-source label material — specifically DailyMed ingredient disclosures — and preserved cleaner or contradictory formulations whenever they surfaced during the audit.

The central operating rule was: do not assume from category reputation; do not dismiss on first impression. Verify on labels. Preserve counterexamples. Narrow the claim only as much as the evidence requires.

Uncertainty was treated as information rather than as a defect to conceal. Rows where the retained research record did not contain individual label-level disclosure are marked plainly rather than filled with invented specificity.

---

## Classification Model

Each row was assigned to one of the following publication-safe categories:

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Strong direct positive** | Repeated label evidence converged on the same active or excipient pattern across the relevant OTC presentation |
| **Formulation-dependent positive** | Row closed positive, but ingredient profile varied by dosage form, manufacturer, or product family |
| **Not individually audited in retained excerpt set** | Row was in scope but individual label discussion was not preserved in this exported artifact |

## Findings: Formulation-Dependent Positives

The following rows closed positive with a required qualification — the ingredient class appears in at least some marketed OTC formulations, while other surfaced products remained simpler or materially different:

- **Cromolyn sodium (NasalCrom):** Benzalkonium chloride and edetate disodium surfaced on DailyMed nasal spray labels
- **Calcium carbonate (Tums):** Positive on some chewable formulations; universalization not supported
- **Bismuth subsalicylate (Pepto-Bismol):** Positive on some surfaced labels; preserve formulation variation
- **Loperamide (Imodium A-D):** Combination forms and some product presentations positive; simpler loperamide-only counterexamples preserved
- **Simethicone (Gas-X, Mylicon):** Standalone infant gas drops surfaced polysorbate 60
- **Bisacodyl (Dulcolax):** Delayed-release tablets positive; suppositories are materially different
- **Sennosides/senna (Senokot, Ex-Lax):** Positive on some surfaced products; formulation caveat required
- **Magnesium hydroxide (Milk of Magnesia):** Some suspensions listed propylene glycol, HPMC, parabens; cleaner counterexample also surfaced
- **Glycerin suppositories (Fleet):** Some products listed polyethylene glycol 400; other products were simpler
- **Clotrimazole, Miconazole nitrate, Tolnaftate:** Closed positive with product variation explicitly preserved
- **Butenafine hydrochloride (Lotrimin Ultra):** Polyoxyethylene cetyl ether and related excipients surfaced

---

## Counterevidence Preserved

The audit deliberately kept counterexamples rather than suppressing them. The most important instances:

- Simpler loperamide-only presentations versus more complex loperamide combination products
- A cleaner magnesium hydroxide surfaced snippet versus other suspensions with more elaborate excipient systems
- Bisacodyl suppositories are materially simpler than the enteric-coated tablet presentations
- Some glycerin suppository formulations were simpler than others

Those counterexamples narrow the wording of the claim but do not dissolve the core pattern.

---

## Publication-Safe Conclusion

The most defensible public statement this audit supports is:

> A broad set of ordinary U.S. OTC products contain petrochemical-linked ingredients at the label level — sometimes as active substances, more often as solvents, preservatives, polymers, coating agents, propellants, or related excipients. The strength and generality of this pattern depends on exact formulation, dosage form, and manufacturer, and careless universalization is not supported. The evidence does support a serious, evidence-grounded pattern claim across a wide product landscape.

**Confidence:** High for the pattern claim. Moderate-to-high for individual rows marked strong direct positive. Qualified for rows marked formulation-dependent positive.
